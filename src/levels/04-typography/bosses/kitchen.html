Brown butter plum cake

Serves 8. One hour and ten minutes.

My grandmother made this cake every September, when the plum tree at the end of her garden gave more fruit than anyone could eat. Browning the butter first gives the crumb a toasted, nutty taste that stands up to the sharp fruit.

Any plums work, but small dark ones hold their shape best. If yours are very ripe, cut them into quarters rather than halves so they don't sink to the bottom of the tin.

Method

Heat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan) and line a 23cm round tin with baking paper. Melt 175g butter in a small pan over a medium heat and let it bubble until it smells of toffee and the solids at the bottom turn golden brown, about five minutes. Pour it into a bowl and leave it to cool for ten minutes.

Whisk 150g light brown sugar and 3 eggs into the butter until smooth. Fold in 175g plain flour, 1½ teaspoons baking powder, 75g ground almonds and a pinch of salt. The batter will be thick, and that is what holds the fruit up.

Spread the batter into the tin. Press 8 to 10 halved plums into the top, cut side up, and scatter a tablespoon of demerara sugar over them.

Bake for 45 to 50 minutes, until a skewer pushed into the middle comes out clean. Leave the cake in the tin for 15 minutes before you turn it out. It keeps for three days in a tin, and it is better on the second.
