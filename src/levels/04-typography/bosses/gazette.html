The last ferry: the river crossing closes in March after ninety years

By Ruth Hallam

The Wrenfield ferry will make its last crossing on Saturday 28 March, the county council confirmed on Tuesday. The service has carried passengers between Mill Quay and the Saltings since 1935, first under oar, then under sail, and since 1971 in the diesel launch Margery, which the council says now needs a new hull it cannot afford.

Passenger numbers have fallen by half in ten years. Most of the people who still use the ferry are walkers on the coast path and a handful of commuters who cycle to the station on the far bank. Without it, the shortest route between the two quays is a 14-mile drive round the head of the estuary.

A group of residents has asked the council to lease the Margery to a community trust for one pound a year. They say a new hull would cost about £40,000 and that a summer timetable run by volunteers could cover the insurance. The council has agreed to hear the proposal at its meeting on 19 November.

Alan Prowse, who has worked the crossing for twenty-six years, says he will be at that meeting. "People think a river is a wall," he said. "It isn't. It's a road, as long as somebody keeps a boat on it."
